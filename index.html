Hepatitis B

The Disease Caused by the Hepatitis B Virus

1. Illness & Organism

Illness: Hepatitis B is a serious infection that affects the liver. It can be acute or chronic and may lead to long-term liver damage.

Common name: Hepatitis B
Scientific name: Hepatitis B virus (HBV)

2. Anatomy of the Virus

  • HBV is a tiny enveloped DNA virus.
  • It has an outer lipid envelope and surface proteins.
  • The core contains viral DNA and protective proteins.
  • It mainly infects liver cells called hepatocytes.

3. When & Where It Is Prevalent

Hepatitis B is found worldwide, but it is most common in parts of sub-Saharan Africa, East Asia, and the Western Pacific. It is also common in some regions of the Middle East and Eastern Europe.

The disease is considered endemic in many of these areas, particularly where vaccination rates are low and healthcare safety is limited.

4. Discovery

Hepatitis B was first identified in the early 1960s. In 1963, Dr. Baruch Blumberg discovered the “Australia antigen,” later identified as the Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg).

This discovery helped scientists understand the virus and contributed to major advances in public health. Blumberg later received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine.

5. Symptoms

  • Fatigue
  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Loss of appetite
  • Abdominal pain
  • Fever
  • Jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes)
  • Dark urine and pale stools

6. Transmission & Life Cycle

  1. The virus enters the bloodstream and attaches to liver cells.
  2. It enters the hepatocyte and releases its DNA into the nucleus.
  3. The infected cell uses viral DNA to make new viral particles.
  4. New viruses are assembled and released.
  5. The cycle continues as the virus infects more liver cells.

HBV is spread through contact with infected blood or body fluids, including unprotected sex, sharing needles, unsafe tattoos or piercings, and mother-to-child transmission during childbirth.

7. Effect on Society

Hepatitis B is a major public health problem because it can lead to cirrhosis, liver failure, and liver cancer.

Many people do not know they are infected, which makes outbreaks harder to control. The disease also increases healthcare costs and affects families, work, and communities.

8. Prevention & Cure

Prevention: vaccination, safe sex, avoiding shared needles, sterilized medical equipment, and blood screening.

Cure: There is no complete cure for everyone, but antiviral treatments such as tenofovir and entecavir can suppress the virus and help prevent liver damage.

9. Interesting Facts

  • Hepatitis B is 100 times more infectious than HIV.
  • Many people show no symptoms for years.
  • Chronic infection is a leading cause of liver cancer worldwide.
  • The hepatitis B vaccine is one of the safest and most effective vaccines.
